# m8 — Tradeoffs & when AI is wrong

## L1 — There's rarely one right answer: naming tradeoffs

**Concept.** Most build decisions have several valid answers, each better at some things and worse at others — simpler vs. more flexible, faster to build vs. faster to run, cheap vs. scalable. "Best" depends on *your* goal. The real skill is naming the tradeoff out loud, so you choose with your eyes open instead of accepting the first option the AI offers as if it were the only one.

**ELI5.** Like choosing a vehicle: bike, car, or truck. None is "best" in the abstract — it depends whether you're commuting, road-tripping, or moving house.

**Deep dive (optional).** For a brand-new build (0→1) you usually weight *simplicity, reversibility, and speed* over raw scale; a product serving millions (1→100) flips toward *reliability and cost control*. Your learner builds are almost always 0→1 — so naming "we're optimizing for getting it working and reversible" makes the simple choice a deliberate one, not a lazy one.

**Build-task tie-in.** For the tracker's storage we'll name the tradeoff — a simple local/file store now vs. a full database — and pick the simple one *on purpose*, knowing what we traded away.

**Check.** Learner can say that decisions have tradeoffs and can name one for a real choice.

---

## L2 — When *not* to let the AI decide

**Concept.** Some decisions you never hand fully to the AI: anything touching **security** (auth, permissions), **real user data** (especially deleting or sharing it), **money** (payments, spending), and **irreversible actions** (deploying to the public, dropping a database, sending a mass email). The AI can advise and prepare; *you* decide and click. This is the same safety seam as the teaching contract: consequential, can't-undo actions belong to the human.

**ELI5.** Autopilot can fly the plane, but a human keeps a hand near the controls for takeoff, landing, and anything that can't be undone.

**Build-task tie-in.** When the tracker gets anywhere near storing personal data or going live, dronAIcharya lays out the options and *stops* for your decision — it never silently ships or deletes on your behalf.

**Under the hood.** These are the "decisions of consequence" the teaching contract reserves for the human. Remember m1: the AI predicts *plausible* actions — and plausible is not the same as safe, especially for things you can't take back.

**Check.** Learner can name the categories where the human must stay the decider (security, data, money, irreversibles).

---

## L3 — Keeping yourself in the loop on decisions of consequence

**Concept.** Staying in the loop means you *understand* each consequential change before it happens — not rubber-stamping it. The practical habits: make the AI explain a risky change in plain language first; do consequential things in small, reversible steps; keep a commit before anything scary; and never approve an action you can't restate in your own words. If you can't explain it, don't approve it yet.

**ELI5.** Don't sign a contract you haven't read just because the salesperson seems nice and is in a hurry.

**Build-task tie-in.** Before any delete-everything step or deploy on the tracker, we pause: here's what it does, here's the undo, do you want it? You answer before it happens.

**Under the hood.** This is why dronAIcharya shows each tool call and stops at real forks. The guardrail isn't a cage — it's a nudge to keep you the author of the decisions that matter, so the finished app is genuinely yours.

**Check (mastery bar for m8).** Learner can name a decision they'd never fully hand to the AI, and explain why (can't undo it / security / real data / money).
